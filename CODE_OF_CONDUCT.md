# Code of Conduct

This project is small and mostly maintained by one person, but it still gets visited, forked, discussed, and sometimes contributed to. The goal of this code of conduct is simple: keep the space usable for the people who are actually trying to build and learn here.

## Our expectations

Be decent. That covers most situations.

In practice, that means:
- Treat other contributors, users, and maintainers with the same courtesy you would want in a professional setting
- Keep feedback on code, docs, and ideas focused on the work
- Assume good faith until there is a real reason not to
- Respect the time of people who are helping, including when they say no or not right now
- Use language that is appropriate for a learning and work-oriented space

## Behavior we do not want

We do not want harassment, threats, insults, discrimination, or anything that makes people less safe or less willing to participate.

That includes:
- Personal attacks instead of technical feedback
- Repeated unwanted contact after someone has asked to stop
- Sharing private information without permission
- Trolling, baiting, or derailing a thread on purpose
- Bringing outside conflicts into the project space
- Using the project to pressure, embarrass, or target someone

This applies in issues, pull requests, discussions, chat, and any other project space.

## Reporting

If something happens that should not have, please report it. You can do that through the project's issue tracker when the matter is not sensitive, or through a private channel when it is.

A good report has enough detail to understand what happened and when, without dragging other people through it. If you are reporting on someone else's behalf, say so.

## What happens after a report

The maintainer will look at the report and decide what to do. Possible responses range from a quiet conversation to removing access to project spaces, depending on what happened.

We are not running a public arbitration forum. The aim is to protect the project and the people in it, not to stage a debate about every report.

## Scope

This code of conduct applies to project spaces and to behavior that affects the project or its community. It does not replace the law, your workplace rules, or your own judgment in situations outside the project.

## A note on tone

Small projects work best when people are direct without being harsh. If you disagree, say what you disagree with and why. If you are frustrated, say that too, but do not make the work feel unsafe or hostile. We can do both honesty and basic respect.
